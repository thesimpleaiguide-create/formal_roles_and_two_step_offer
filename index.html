Formal roles and the two-step offer

Follow the 4 sequential stages of the NHS chaperoning pathway, then explore role standards below.

Step 1 — At booking or when the need is identified

Explain that a chaperone can be provided, record the patient's request or preference where appropriate, and make arrangements early enough to avoid unnecessary delay or embarrassment.

?

Who can be a formal chaperone?

A formal chaperone should be appropriately trained, suitable for the role, DBS-checked where required by local arrangements, impartial, aware of their safeguarding responsibilities, and present throughout the examination.
✓

Respecting preferences

Patients may have preferences relating to sex, religion, culture, trauma history, disability, communication needs, or privacy. Staff should respond sensitively, consider reasonable adjustments, and avoid penalising a patient for requesting a safe alternative.
!

Urgent exceptions

In a genuinely life-threatening emergency, immediate clinical care may need to take priority. The reason for proceeding without usual arrangements should be clearly recorded in the medical notes.
